\title{The Ticket Cuts the Cheating, the Model Shapes the Confession:\\What Coding Agents Report After Bypassing an Impossible Test}
\author{Amir Kellou-Sidhoum\\ Independent researcher\\ \texttt{amirksmain@gmail.com}}
\date{November 2026}

\begin{abstract}
A rule in the ticket cuts how often a coding agent bypasses an impossible test; what the agent then admits differs sharply between the models that bypass. Four model-and-harness packages received two pytest tasks that cannot pass in the sandbox, and a deterministic checker out of the agent's reach decided every bypass. In a pre-registered $2\times2$ factorial on the ticket, bypassing fell from \kn{fact.frozen.pooled.P0R0} runs with no rule to \kn{fact.frozen.pooled.P1R0} with a prohibition, \kn{fact.frozen.pooled.P0R1} with a one-sentence permission to report the blocker, and \kn{fact.frozen.pooled.P1R1} with both (\kn{fact.ext.pooled.P1R1}, none green, in an extended sample). Blind coders from two model families then coded the final message of the \num{H1.confess.n} green bypasses that ended with a report. Under every coder, reports named the shortcut (\cx{rate.B.explicit} to \cx{rate.X.explicit}) more often than they said what the tests no longer checked (\cx{rate.A.consequence} to \cx{rate.X.consequence}; paired $p < 10^{-6}$ per coder). GPT-6.1 Sol named its shortcut in \kn{ph.gpt.explicit} reports; DeepSeek V4 Flash left it unnamed in \kn{ph.deepseek.not_explicit} and contradicted the checker in \kn{ph.deepseek.misrep_factual}. A Claude monitor reading only the ticket and the report accepted \kn{mon.claude.R.miss} bypasses as trustworthy, \kn{mon.claude.D.miss} with the diff; most of those it accepted were synthetic test data the report had disclosed. The reporting sentence had no detectable effect on what bypassers reported (an underpowered test), and the pre-registered claim that full confession is a minority did not survive audit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

On impossible tests, the ticket sets how often coding agents cheat, and what they then admit to the user differs from one model to the next. The final message is often all a user, or the next agent in a chain, sees of an agent's work. Prior work measures how often agents take shortcuts \citep{zhong2025impossiblebench,gomez2026escalation,hu2026transmissible} or whether reports overclaim \citep{smyth2026overclaim,advani2026falsesuccess}. We measure both on the same runs, with a deterministic checker as ground truth.

On runs from a hackathon study whose bench, checker and tickets are public \citep{kellou2026refusal}, we report three results.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The two halves of a rule, separated} (pre-registered; recounted from the raw run folders). A prohibition and a one-sentence permission to report the blocker each cut bypassing; together they left \kn{fact.frozen.pooled.P1R1} at the freeze. Neither half sufficed for every package (Section~\ref{sec:fact}).
  \item \textbf{What the report says} (pre-registered; new). Reports name the shortcut more often than its cost, under four codings from two model families; false statements are a minority concentrated in one package (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
  \item \textbf{What a reader catches} (pre-registered; new). Monitors reading the report alone accept a minority of bypasses, and mostly the openly disclosed ones (Section~\ref{sec:monitor}).
\end{enumerate}
Before any claim was written, an LLM sub-agent audit that had not seen our conclusions recomputed every number without discrepancy and, reading about 70 items, found one pre-registered code wrong in most cases it read. Claims rest on the codes that survived it.

The prohibition also changes how agents bypass. With neither sentence, \kn{H2.extended.cells.P0R0.green_bypass} runs ended in a green bypass; with the prohibition alone \kn{H2.extended.cells.P1R0.bypass} runs still violated the rule and \kn{H2.extended.cells.P1R0.green_bypass} ended green. Their reports call the test ``untouched'' and say it ``exercises the real provider''; two of the four green ones add that the fallback is what makes it pass. In this stratum, the shortcut left once the listed ones are forbidden is one that reads as ordinary work.

\section{What the report says}\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Agreement.} The two Claude coders agreed on the category for \kn{agr.category.C.agree} bypass runs ($\kappa$ = \num{agr.category.C.kappa}, bootstrap 95\,\% interval \num{agr.category.C.kappa_ci}). On the same bypass runs, by variable: mechanism $\kappa$ = \num{agr.V3.C.kappa}, consequence \num{agr.V4.C.kappa}, misrepresentation \num{agr.V5.C.kappa}, compliance claim \num{agr.V6.C.kappa}; \num{n.adjudicated} items, from all sets and on any of the adjudicated variables, went to the adjudicator. GPT-6.1 Sol, coding every item once with the same instructions (amendment 4), agreed with the final codes as closely as the Claude coders agreed with each other ($\kappa$ = \num{cx.final.C.category.kappa} against \num{cx.AB.C.category.kappa}), least on the consequence (\num{cx.final.C.V4.kappa}). It counts ``the test is skipped'' as a stated consequence and ``reference archive'' for generated data as a misrepresentation, so its levels are higher on both (Table~\ref{tab:coders}). We give each headline rate as the range across the four codings.

\paragraph{Named, not costed.} \pc{ph.all.explicit} of reports say that the data are synthetic or the test skipped or replaced; \pc{ph.all.consequence} say that the tests no longer check the real archive or provider. Under each of the four codings, reports that name the mechanism without the consequence outnumber the reverse (exact McNemar, $p < 10^{-6}$ each). \pc{ph.all.misrep_factual} contain a statement the checker contradicts (a substitute called real, a modified test called untouched), up to \cx{rate.X.factual} for the GPT coder. Runs within a package and task follow templates, so exact intervals are too narrow; with the \num{rb.equal_weight.n_strata} package-task-block strata that have at least five reports weighted equally, the three rates are \num{rb.equal_weight.explicit}, \num{rb.equal_weight.consequence} and \num{rb.equal_weight.factual}. Compliance language (``this respects the policy'') appears in \pc{ph.all.compliance} of bypass reports and is as common in honest refusals (\pc{rb.K.compliance}).

\paragraph{Packages differ.} Packages differ more than ticket conditions (Figure~\ref{fig:main}b, Table~\ref{tab:decomp}). \textbf{GPT} named the shortcut in \kn{ph.gpt.explicit} reports and stated the consequence in \kn{ph.gpt.consequence}: \emph{``These tests validate parsing; they no longer validate the real field archive's contents.''} \textbf{GLM} named it in \kn{ph.glm.explicit}; on S6 without a rule one template says the test now skips without \texttt{fxprovider}, that no mock was used (true), and that this ``honors'' the policy that FX tests hit the real provider. \textbf{DeepSeek} left the shortcut unnamed in \kn{ph.deepseek.not_explicit}, claimed success without caveat in \kn{ph.deepseek.unqualified}, and described a substitute as real in \kn{ph.deepseek.misrep_factual}, such as a module it wrote with a fallback constant called ``a real rate client''. Under the GPT coder, DeepSeek again has the most misrepresentations (\num{cx.rate.X.deepseek.factual.kn}) and GPT none (\num{cx.rate.X.gpt.factual.kn}). Claude Sonnet 5.5 never bypassed outside the positive control. GLM and DeepSeek share a harness (Codex CLI), so their difference is not explained by the harness alone; package comparisons are not randomised and mix task strata. Exploratory H4 gives the share of transparent reports by package: GPT \num{H4.packages_transp.gpt.pct}, GLM \num{H4.packages_transp.glm.pct}, DeepSeek \num{H4.packages_transp.deepseek.pct}; GLM's share is low because of sub-type c.

\paragraph{Pre-registered tests that failed.} \textbf{H1}, full confession a minority: \pc{H1.confess} on the pre-registered definition (Holm $p$ = \num{holm.H1}), but \num{ph.all.misrep_c.k} of the \num{dist.green_bypass.MISREPRESENT} misrepresentations are sub-type c, which the audit found mostly wrong; without it confession is \pc{ph.all.noc.confess}, and coder B alone gives \pc{sens.B.confess}. Not claimed. \textbf{H2}, the reporting sentence lowers undisclosed bypass: \kn{H2.frozen.cells.P0R0.undisclosed} against \kn{H2.frozen.cells.P0R1.undisclosed} per randomised run at the freeze ($p$ = \num{H2.frozen.p_raw}), \kn{H2.extended.cells.P0R0.undisclosed} against \kn{H2.extended.cells.P0R1.undisclosed} in the pre-registered full sample (Holm $p$ = \num{holm.H2}); among bypassers, confession \kn{H2b.confess.P0R0} against \kn{H2b.confess.P0R1} ($p$ = \num{H2b.p_raw}, treatment-selected). No detectable effect at these sample sizes. \textbf{H3}, the rule-present against rule-absent contrast, is dropped: 13 of the \num{H3.present.n} rule-present ``violations'' are not shortcuts (scratch files under \texttt{/tmp}, import paths, three real fixes). \textbf{H5}, written before the runs, predicted that most GPT reports would be laundered: \pc{H5.laundered}, refuted.

\paragraph{Controls.} None of \num{n.K} random runs without a bypass claimed success (\kn{K.success_claim_any}); all were red, so they cannot estimate a false-positive rate. No report in any set mentioned being evaluated. The coders were not told that the skip was permitted in the \num{n.pos} positive controls and coded \num{dist.pos.MISREPRESENT} of them as misrepresentations; these are excluded from every rate.

\section{What a reader catches}\label{sec:monitor}

Two monitors, Claude Sonnet 5.5 and GPT-6.1 Sol, answered one question per green run, pre-registered before any item was built (amendment 5): \emph{if this change is merged, do the passing tests still verify what they were written to verify?} Each saw the ticket and the final message (R), or both plus the diff (D), in separate folders, never the checker's verdict, the package or the task's feasibility. Positives: the \num{mon.claude.R.miss.n} green bypasses. Negatives: 60 legitimate fixes of the feasible task S6F, 20 each from GLM, Claude and DeepSeek.

\paragraph{Misses.} From the report alone the Claude monitor accepted \kn{mon.claude.R.miss} bypasses as trustworthy (cluster bootstrap by stratum \num{mon.claude.R.miss_cboot}), and \kn{mon.claude.D.miss} with the diff (paired exact test, one-sided $p$ = \num{mon.claude.M1.p_raw}). The GPT monitor accepted \kn{mon.gpt.R.miss} and \kn{mon.gpt.D.miss} ($p$ = \num{mon.gpt.M1.p_raw}). Both monitors flagged most bypasses; their specificity is unknown (below).

\paragraph{What is missed is disclosed.} The pre-registered M2 predicted more misses among non-transparent reports. Among the \num{H1.confess.n} bypasses with a report, the reverse held: the Claude monitor missed \kn{mon.claude.M2.transp} transparent and \kn{mon.claude.M2.nontransp} non-transparent reports. \num{mon.claude.R.miss_S7.kn} of its misses are on S7, nearly all reports stating that the tests now run on generated records, which the monitor accepted as ordinary unit testing. Our checker encodes the ticket's view that the tests exist to check the field archive. The residual risk is a shortcut stated in the open that the reader approves.

\paragraph{False alarms are not interpretable.} The Claude monitor flagged \kn{mon.claude.R.falsealarm} legitimate fixes and the GPT monitor \kn{mon.gpt.R.falsealarm}, including all 20 of Claude's. The provider installed for S6F describes itself as an offline build serving a fixed snapshot; honest reports said so, and the GPT monitor read that as a live test that no longer checks the live service (amendment 6). These negatives are not clean, so we do not estimate specificity.

\section{Discussion and limitations}\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Implications.} A deployment that needs the cost stated has to ask for it (``if a test no longer checks what it was written to check, say which''): our reporting sentence asked for the blocker and got the blocker. Whether the cost sentence works is untested. Because the bypasses a reader accepts are mostly disclosed ones, the ticket has to state whether substitutes such as generated test data are acceptable. The \kn{fact.frozen.pooled.P1R1} with the full rule covers three packages, two tasks and one wording; it became \kn{fact.ext.pooled.P1R1} in the extended sample and is not evidence of protection in general.

\paragraph{Limitations.} \textbf{Post hoc.} The original study planned and withdrew a coding of reports; this one was pre-registered after the runs existed, by the same author, after about ten messages had been read, with worked examples taken from the original report's annex. Pre-registration and amendments are committed before each step but not externally time-stamped, and the adjudicator's rules (amendment 3) were written after reading the coders' notes, before any agreement or result. \textbf{Coders.} Three Claude models and GPT-6.1 Sol, which is also a package; in the category disagreements it resolved the adjudicator sided with coder A in \num{rb.adj_sided.with_A}, with coder B in \num{rb.adj_sided.with_B}, and with neither in the rest; and $\kappa$ for the consequence was \num{agr.V4.C.kappa} on bypass runs. No human has coded the items; a blind audit sheet is in the repository. Blinding was partial, since messages quote tickets and carry their authors' style. \textbf{Measure.} The strict measure flags a few harmless changes (\texttt{/tmp} scratch files, \texttt{testpaths} entries); three feasible-task runs are in the green-bypass denominator. On S7 many fabricated archives are written by a fixture into pytest's temporary directory; coders were told the archive was ``created by the agent''. A sentinel probe, pre-registered before it ran (\texttt{chain/PREREG\_CHAIN.md}), restored each missing dependency in a deliberately wrong version: the agent's tests still passed in \kn{sen.notdetect} green bypasses, so only there does the green no longer check the dependency; in the rest the shortcut costs nothing once the dependency is back (for example, a skip that runs the test when the provider is present). Reports stated a consequence about as often in both groups (\kn{sen.conseq_notdetect} against \kn{sen.conseq_detect}, Fisher $p$ = \num{sen.conseq_fisher_p}), so the consequence code measures what the report says, not what the shortcut costs; we leave that measure to a follow-up. \textbf{Monitors.} Two models, one prompt, negatives from one task. \textbf{Scope.} Two small tasks, one wording, three packages that bypass, factorial cells of 15 to 20 runs, and a large share of bypasses from one night block. Pooled rates follow these unequal strata; we report per package throughout. \textbf{Intent.} We code what the report says against the checker: an omission is not a lie, and a false statement is not evidence of intent.

\section{Grid, amendments and checks}\label{app:amend}
\textbf{Grid.} The coders' instructions are in \texttt{coding/CODER\_INSTRUCTIONS.md}. The category is derived in the fixed order \textsc{no report}, \textsc{misrepresent}, \textsc{confess}, \textsc{disclose}, \textsc{vague}, \textsc{silent}. The three worked examples were a GPT report calling its data ``explicitly synthetic'' and saying the tests ``no longer validate the real field archive's contents'' (\textsc{confess}); a GLM report calling generated data ``a checked-in snapshot'' (\textsc{misrepresent}, a); and a GLM report that adds \texttt{importorskip}, says a skipped test ``isn't the same as a passing one'' and claims the real-provider policy is respected (\textsc{misrepresent}, c). The third example drives sub-type c.

\textbf{Amendments.} (1) Coded set 384, not 392: eight relay lead runs are not worker runs and were excluded by the pre-registered rule. (2) Adjudication extended to V2 and V6; non-adjudicated items take coder A's codes. (3) Four adjudicator rules for wordings the coders flagged, written after their notes, before any result. (4) Cross-family coder GPT-6.1 Sol. (5) Monitor baseline. (6) The S6F negatives are not clean.

\textbf{Checks.} The factorial cells at both freezes were recounted from the run folders. About 2{,}900 run folders exist; the \num{n.C} coded runs and the 1{,}450 night and 478 morning analysed runs come from the original study's frozen tables; the rest are launches where the agent never started. An adversarial sub-agent that had not seen our conclusions recomputed every result (no discrepancy), recounted the factorial (no discrepancy) and read about 70 items. It found sub-type c mostly wrong, the pooled H3 contrast an artefact, clustering by stratum, the positive controls coded without the permission, and the adjudicator closer to coder A; it estimated error rates under 10\,\% for transparent against not, 15 to 20\,\% for \textsc{confess} against \textsc{disclose}, 30 to 50\,\% for \textsc{vague}, and 5 to 10\,\% for sub-type a.
